Research Interests

Presently, my research interests include the following:

1. Localization in Quantum Many-Body Systems

The Nobel-winning theory of Anderson localization is a central topic in dynamical systems and spectral theory. Originally formulated for a single particle in a disordered medium, it has since been extended to nonlinear and few-body systems. However, a rigorous mathematical theory of localization in the genuine many-body setting—where the number of particles tends to infinity—remains elusive. Resolving this problem is crucial for understanding new physical phenomena observed in recent laboratory experiments.

2. Operator Kernel Estimates

Heat kernels and resolvents are ubiquitous in analysis. Their estimates play an important role in harmonic, microlocal, and geometric analysis, and are among the most studied examples of a broader concept known as operator kernel estimates. Given a self-adjoint operator and a sufficiently regular real function, the operator kernel can be defined as a natural generalization of the classical kernel function of an integral operator. We seek to develop further operator-theoretic techniques for studying the decay properties of general operator kernels on graphs and manifolds.

3. Nonlinear Partial Differential Equations

Consider nonlinear evolution equations that admit large families of soliton solutions, such as the Allen–Cahn and Ginzburg–Landau equations, as well as many geometric flows. These solitons form lower-dimensional structures in the configuration space. Our goal is to derive corresponding lower-dimensional effective dynamics that approximate the full evolution near these solitons. Such effective dynamics typically involve far fewer degrees of freedom and may be viewed as a rigorous form of renormalization.

4. Applied Analysis

Many problems arising in engineering, including control systems and signal processing, can be formulated mathematically as systems of differential equations. Practical objectives often reduce to deriving suitable a priori estimates for these equations. We aim to extract mathematically meaningful problems from real-world applications, including optics, metrology, and robotics, and to develop the analytical tools needed to address them under realistic constraints.
